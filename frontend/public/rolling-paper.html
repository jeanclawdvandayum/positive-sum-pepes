I. Design goals

The anti memecoin,
memecoin.

01

The launchpad routine is familiar. A KOL sells you a fresh chart, takes an early position, and dumps into the attention they brought in. Then comes another ticker and the same sales pitch. Positive Sum Pepes is a direct response to that cycle, built around people who want to play together instead of buying the next round of launchpad slop.

The opening is an initial bonding curve offering, or IBCO. Willing participants pool their deposits for one first purchase of PSP, and each receives a share of that purchase in proportion to their deposit. Everyone in the pool enters through the same trade. A larger deposit buys a larger position, while deposit order within the window gives no price advantage.

That ground-floor entry comes with a commitment. The opening PSP becomes lePSP, or locked earning PSP, held in a Pepe NFT. Requesting withdrawal starts the four-week schedule. While locked, the position earns a share of trading fees. Detonation releases every lock early. The epoch boundaries and exact waiting times appear in the staking section.

Each mechanism has a job

The IBCO sends 10% of the pooled mixETH to a countdown lottery pot and uses the remaining 90% for the opening curve purchase. Later buyers receive PSP and can take spots on the ladder. The ten most recent tickets split the pot when the round ends, while each whole ticket adds up to 69 seconds to the timer. Taking a seat gives everyone else more time to take yours.

Buys and sells pay a fee that moves inversely with curve reserves. Low reserves mean higher fees, reaching 10%. Higher reserves bring the fee down to 2.5%. Of each referred trade fee, 60% goes to PSP stakers, 35% to the pot and 5% to the referral chain. The fee section explains the thresholds and the alternative split for trades without a referral.

The game takes human greed seriously. Some participants want trading gains, some want a stream of fees, and some want to take the pot. Others simply want a Pepe they like. Each of these motives brings a different kind of participation into the system. The design question is how to make those actions useful to the people on the other side of them.

THE GAME IN ONE PASSThe full mechanics ↓
  1. Pool the opening.

    The three-day IBCO combines uncapped deposits into one PSP purchase. Ten percent seeds the countdown pot.

  2. Lock the early positions.

    The opening PSP becomes lePSP. It stays locked until a withdrawal request starts the four-week schedule, or detonation opens every lock.

  3. Buy a place on the ladder.

    The ten most recent tickets share the pot. Each whole ticket adds up to 69 seconds, within the 69:04:20 maximum clock.

  4. Let trading pay the participants.

    Both buys and sells pay a sliding 10% to 2.5% fee. The referred split is 60% stakers, 35% pot, 5% referrals.

  5. Put a Pepe on the position.

    Every staked position comes with generative art. There are 191,664,000 combinations, almost 200 million ways to look ridiculous.

  6. Settle when the clock reaches zero.

    Trading stops. Detonation settles the ladder pot, opens every lock and flattens the curve. PSP redeems against remaining reserves with a 0% trading fee.

  7. Start again with the same people.

    Anyone can help spawn the next round. It starts from another IBCO with a new PSP token, fresh curve and separate backing.

After detonation, redemption uses proportional backing per PSP instead of the live curve. Integer rounding can slightly change the exact rate. Old-round winnings, backing and earned fees remain claimable.

Where the positive sum comes from

The interaction between traders, stakers, and pot competitors creates activity, but activity alone is an internal redistribution of money. A fee paid by a trader becomes income for a staker or another recipient. Adding these transfers together leaves the group with the same value before external costs. For the system to gain value as a whole, something has to earn outside it.

This is where Alchemix’s mixETH comes in. mixETH is an ETH vault token that uses the ERC-4626 standard, allowing its underlying capital to earn yield outside the game. As that yield increases the vault’s ETH redemption value, the same amount of mixETH can represent more ETH. The game continues to count deposits, fees, backing, and payouts in mixETH, while the asset beneath those balances can become more valuable in ETH terms.

ΔVcollective = external return − external costs − lossesConceptual value bridge, measured in a common external unit. Internal transfers cancel when summed across all recipients.
(1)

That external income is the basis for calling the game positive sum. The engine brings participants together and routes their payments according to the game’s rules, while the backing asset provides a potential source of value from outside those exchanges. Whether the group gains overall depends on that income exceeding external costs and losses. Individual outcomes still depend on entry price, trading decisions, fees, and the performance of mixETH itself.

II. Round stages

One round.
Four stages.

02

PSP runs on Uniswap v4 in repeating rounds. Each round starts with its own PSP token, mixETH backing, bonding curve, and countdown. The lifecycle gives participants a common opening, a period of active competition, and a defined way to settle before they decide whether to return.

↳Pool the launch deposits.

Public predeposits fund one pooled purchase at launch. Each depositor can claim a share of its PSP in a separate staked position.

↗Trade and earn fees.

Participants buy and sell through the curve. Trades pay fees. Whole purchase units add tickets and time.

×Settle the round.

At zero, the contract stops trading. Detonation settles the pot and permits immediate withdrawal from every staked position.

↻Create the next round.

Anyone can help create the next round. Each old round keeps its backing and unpaid claims.

The IBCO: a common opening purchase

The predeposit window is open to anyone who wants to participate. Deposits combine into one pooled purchase, and each depositor receives a proportional share of the PSP it buys. This removes the advantage of ordering deposits within the window, though a larger deposit still buys a larger share. The IBCO lasts three days and accepts any positive deposit. Total deposits and individual wallets are uncapped under the default configuration. At launch, 10% of the pooled mixETH funds the pot and the remaining 90% becomes curve backing.

Participants also select their Pepe during the deposit action. The transaction reserves that art for the round, and further deposits add to the same reservation. The Pepe is an NFT that will hold the depositor’s staked position, combining the financial claim and the collectible in one asset.

Once the pooled purchase completes, depositors can claim their chosen Pepe with their share of staked PSP and earned fees. That reservation survives detonation, so a late claim keeps the same art. The contract rejects a conflicting reservation if either the identifier or its art combination is already reserved or minted in the round.

Early entry comes with a lock

The pooled PSP enters indefinitely locked positions. This balances the shared opening price with a commitment to the game. This locked earning PSP is called lePSP. The standard four-week withdrawal schedule begins on request. Early participants earn staking fees while committed, and detonation opens the lock immediately.

From the IBCO to an active round

The predeposit window lasts three days. Anyone can launch after the window ends. Launch starts a countdown of 69 hours, 4 minutes and 20 seconds. This is also the maximum time a purchase can restore. These transitions require transactions from callers. The passage of time makes an action available, and someone then submits it.

launch ops

Contracts do not wake themselves up. Enthusiasm does not get an exemption from arithmetic.

Assets and units

PSP is the token participants buy, stake, sell, and eventually redeem. lePSP names the locked earning PSP held in an NFT position. Staking keeps the same underlying PSP, and withdrawal releases that PSP. Purchases mint it, while sales and redemption burn it. mixETH remains the common unit across the system, allowing the curve, the fee accounts, and the pot to settle in the same asset. A price expressed in mixETH therefore measures something different from a dollar price.

A small deposit can pass the deposit check while the pooled purchase remains too small for the contract’s arithmetic. A small depositor’s PSP share can also round to zero.

III. Price and supply

A curve that gives
the market room to move.

03

A typical market has areas where capital accumulates with relatively little price movement and areas where a smaller amount of capital moves the price sharply. PSP builds that variation into a tilted sine bonding curve. As reserves increase, the curve passes through gentler and steeper sections, giving traders different execution conditions along the way. Selling moves reserves back along the same curve. The shape determines the quoted price, while participants determine whether capital moves into or out of it.

The price equation

Let R be the curve reserve. Let b be the reserve just after launch fees, and let B be the price at that reserve. Below b, the price follows an exponential curve. Above b, a sine term changes the slope of the logarithmic price trend.

The x-axis is reserves. Your very red Tuesday remains entirely possible.

p(R) = B exp[s(R − b) − A sin(2π(R − b)/λ)]After boot. B = 7.5p₀, k = ln(7.5)/b, R* = b × 10,000/450, λ = (R* − b)/3, s = ln(P*/B)/(R* − b), A = αsλ/(2π).
(2)

The default p₀ is 0.00001 mixETH per PSP. The gentler IBCO ramp reaches 0.000075 at launch, a 7.5-fold rise. The third wave reaches about 0.06, an 800-fold rise from that launch price. Its reserve target scales with the net IBCO backing. A 50 mixETH IBCO puts it near 1,000 reserves. A 500 mixETH IBCO puts it near 10,000. A 5,000 mixETH IBCO puts it near 100,000. The amplitude α ranges from zero to one. At zero, the logarithmic price follows a straight trend. At one, the curve has points with a zero slope.

d ln p / dR = s[1 − α cos(2π(R − b)/λ)] ≥ 0For the supported amplitude, the price increases or stays constant as reserve increases. A sale decreases reserve.
(3)

The price of the whole trade

A purchase moves through an interval of the curve, so its execution price depends on every part of that interval. As the marginal price rises, each additional mixETH buys less PSP. We express this using q(R), the integral of reciprocal price. The difference between q at the starting and ending reserves gives the ideal PSP output for the purchase.

q(R) = ∫0R 1/p(u) du
Δq = q(R + x − F) − q(R)x is gross mixETH input. F is the trade fee. q is an ideal curve coordinate. Actual token supply also reflects past trades and output reductions.
(4)

The implementation uses fixed numerical integration and a conservative calculation of the reverse trade. It subtracts one wei from the purchase output, then applies a reduction of one basis point. One basis point is 0.01%. Wei is the smallest unit of the token’s 18-decimal accounting.

This formulation makes adjacent reserve intervals add up to the same ideal total as one larger interval. In practice, fees, conservative output reductions, and whole-ticket rounding mean that splitting an order can change its result. The third-wave reserve target scales with each round’s IBCO. The wave continues beyond that target within the supported arithmetic range.

IV. Countdown and pot

The clock creates urgency.
The pot gives it a price.

04

The countdown turns fading activity into a visible deadline. Each whole ticket adds up to 69 seconds to the clock. Each ticket costs one ten-thousandth of the current pot, sampled before a purchase adds its own fees. A 500 mixETH IBCO starts tickets at 0.005; every fee leg grows the pot, and the price with it. A buyer is therefore doing two things at once, taking a place in the competition for the pot and extending the time during which someone else can take that place.

At the 500-mix starting point, a 0.05 mixETH purchase earns ten tickets and adds up to 11 minutes and 30 seconds. After the pot grows by another 100 mixETH, each ticket costs 0.015; after 1,000 more, 0.105. Each buy uses the price before its fees enter the pot. The active purchase minimum equals exactly one current ticket — a buy below it reverts rather than trading ticketless. The countdown stays capped at the round’s maximum.

When the countdown reaches zero, both purchases and sales stop. The remaining ladder becomes the basis for settlement, and anyone can call detonation. A purchase submitted after expiry cannot bring the round back to life. This gives the competition an enforceable endpoint, even if the detonation transaction arrives later.

V. Trade fees

Trading activity becomes
income for participants.

05

The fee changes with the size of the reserve. Active trading starts at 10%, then the rate decreases toward 2.5% as reserves approach the calibration target. The contract sets the rate from the reserve before the trade. This makes the fee part of the market’s progression, with a higher charge near the opening and a lower charge as more capital enters.

f(R) = 10% − 7.5% · clamp((R − b)/(R* − b), 0, 1)Buy fee: f × gross mixETH input. Sell fee: f × gross curve output. The contract interpolates in integer basis points.
(7)

Stakers are the main recipients of that activity. By locking PSP, a participant takes a share of the earning weight and receives fees as other participants trade. This gives someone with a longer horizon a reason to stay involved through the movement of the market, while traders continue to pursue their own opportunities on the curve and the ladder.

Of each active trade fee, 60% goes to stakers, 35% goes to the pot, and 5% funds eligible referrals. If credited referral rewards are zero, that last share becomes another 4% for the pot and 1% in deployer credit. The split describes where the fee goes. A staker’s return still depends on the volume of trading and their share of total earning weight.

Referral attribution

Referrals give participants an incentive to bring other people into the game. The buyer authorizes attribution through a signed action, and the relationship then stays fixed for that round. The referral allocation follows five tiers, distributing 80%, 12%, 5%, 2%, and 1% of that share along the eligible chain.

To establish a new attribution, the selected Pepe’s owner must hold at least 1,000 lePSP. Referral rewards accrue to the current NFT owners, with each owner receiving at most one tier. Each round’s referral registry holds the earned mixETH until the recipient claims it. Earned balances stay with the wallet that earned them after a Pepe transfer, while future rewards follow the new owner. Claims remain open after the round ends. This ties the referral position to an owned asset, while the signed attribution ensures that a supplied address alone is insufficient to bind a buyer.

Separate payment obligations

The fee routes create separate obligations even when funds share a contract address. Curve reserves support PSP redemption, the pot supports ladder claims, and earned credits belong to stakers and the deployer. The system must keep enough mixETH to cover all of these balances. Money already owed to one group remains committed to that group.

VI. NFT positions

PSP is for stakers.
The Pepe holds the position.

06

The staking system borrows from DeFi locking mechanisms and puts the position inside a Pepe NFT. Its principal, withdrawal schedule, and earned fees travel together, so transferring the Pepe also transfers the financial rights attached to it. The collectible becomes the position itself, giving participants something they can hold, build on, and trade.

The NFT holds the position

Withdrawing the principal leaves the NFT in the wallet as a collectible from that round. Any unpaid fees remain attached to it. A Pepe can therefore move from an active earning position into a record of the game its owner played, while preserving any outstanding financial claim.

A fresh stake receives earning weight immediately for later fee events. Keeping it locked preserves its full weight, allowing it to participate in the stream of trading fees. Requesting withdrawal starts a gradual exit. The position keeps its full weight for the request epoch, then loses one sixth at each of the next six epoch boundaries until its weight reaches zero.

This gradual reduction applies to future earnings. Fees already earned stay with the position, allowing someone to leave without giving up the income accrued while their capital was committed.

Accounting checkpoints preserve the weight at each fee event and fractional credit across claims. An explicit claim reverts if payment fails. Some owner actions can defer payment and retain the credit on the NFT. This credit remains after principal withdrawal. An NFT transfer moves these rights to the new owner.

The standard schedule retains six epoch boundaries across four weeks. Each epoch lasts 4 days and 16 hours. Depending on when the request lands, withdrawal takes about 23 days and 8 hours to 28 days. Cancelling the withdrawal restores full weight immediately. If the round detonates, every position becomes available for immediate withdrawal.

Reinvestment completes another cycle within the game. Earned mixETH buys more PSP, and that PSP returns to the same staked position, increasing its principal. The purchase pays the normal trade fee and requires at least 0.005 mixETH. The owner or an approved operator chooses when to execute it, so the decision to compound remains with the participant.

Each reserved or minted art combination stays unavailable to other mints in that round, including after transfer or withdrawal. This release supports 191,664,000 combinations through art version 2. Predeposit users choose their art during the deposit action.

The older fallback derives art from a wallet and the round’s fixed seed. The contract stores each minted Pepe’s DNA, the value that encodes its traits. VII. Settlement

Settle the round.
Keep the community.

07

As activity slows, the clock approaches its endpoint. At zero, trading halts, and detonation moves the game from competition to settlement. It fixes the pot for claims, flattens the curve, and opens every staking lock. The incentive to race other sellers down the live curve gives way to redemption against the remaining backing.

Final ticket holders claim their shares of the pot, while PSP holders can burn their tokens for their shares of the old round’s mixETH backing at zero trade fees. These claims remain open indefinitely, and the owner has no sweep over the funds that support them. The next round starts with separate capital, leaving the previous round’s obligations where participants can still claim them.

mixETH out = ⌊PSP in × Rremaining / Sremaining⌋The payment uses remaining backing divided by remaining supply. Rounding dust stays for the remaining holders. The exact rate per token can therefore change.

A fresh chart for the same people

The usual response to a fading memecoin is another ticker and another launch. PSP makes the restart part of the game. Participants can return with the experience of the previous round, the relationships they formed, and a better understanding of how other people play. A community that wants another market can create one through the same process.

Anyone can help spawn that fresh round. It brings a new PSP token, another IBCO, and separate backing. Its participants decide how much new capital to commit. This allows attention and activity to return without carrying the old round’s financial obligations into the new one.

The chart starts again.
The community brings its experience.

What continues

Trade history, strategies, and relationships carry forward. People can study what happened, decide what they would do differently, and return for another round. The community gets a recurring activity around which to organise, and every new round gives that activity another opening.

What stays with the old round

Backing and unpaid claims remain in their original round. PSP holders retain redemption rights, final ladder holders retain pot claims, and earned staking fees remain payable. Joining the next game requires a fresh deposit or purchase, keeping that decision separate from settling an earlier position.

New round. Separate funds.

Together, these pieces turn the launch cycle into a recurring economic game. Predeposits bring capital in, traders create volume, stakers earn fees, and the pot concentrates attention around a deadline. Settlement closes the competition, and respawn opens the next one. Whether the cycle continues depends on people finding enough value in it to come back.

How to create the next round

Anyone can reserve the next round and complete its three birth steps. Splitting creation across transactions keeps the work within gas limits and allows a caller to resume from an unfinished step. This makes the restart a public process that other participants can help complete.

The full three-day predeposit window starts when the last birth step finishes connecting the new contracts. A long gap between detonation and respawn therefore leaves the next round with its full deposit window. All positions and claims from earlier rounds remain with their original contracts.

VIII. Limits and risks

The tradeoffs
are part of the system.

08

Combining these mechanisms gives participants several ways to engage with the game, and it also gives them several ways to compete with one another. Capital concentration, transaction order, and external yield all affect the outcome. We can define how the game settles, while the people playing it still determine who gains and who loses.

Design properties and limits
What the design doesResults the design does not guarantee

One participant can capture the pot.

One wallet can hold lePSP, receive referral payments, and hold all ten ladder seats. A purchase followed by a sale pays fees but retains tickets. If other buyers leave those tickets in place, a large pot can make the sequence profitable. Transaction order, competition, gas costs, and ownership affect the result. Fees alone cannot guarantee that this strategy loses money.

The factory retains owner controls.

The clock and a public detonation call control the end of a round, without a governance vote. The factory still has owner controls over future curve settings, the descriptor, stored HTML, and cancellation of a spawn reservation. Those administrative powers matter when assessing the system, even though existing round code stays fixed.

mixETH has external risks.

The positive-sum premise depends on the production mixETH asset earning outside the game. That brings the risks of its strategies, custody, liquidity, and returns into the backing asset. PSP settles in mixETH rather than reading an ETH exchange rate, so a loss in the underlying asset can affect the value participants ultimately receive.

Each round has its own parameters.

Each round keeps the code and parameters from its deployment. Factory settings can affect later rounds rather than rewrite an existing round’s code. Check the deployed contracts and their parameters before a transaction.

Arithmetic and execution have limits.

The ideal wave can extend without a fixed endpoint. Solidity arithmetic has finite limits. The contracts bound custom parameters, and a quote can revert outside the supported range. Tests cover specified domains. Contract errors, token failures, transaction order, and chain availability remain risks. Mathematical consistency alone is insufficient evidence of security.
